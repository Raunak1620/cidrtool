What is route summarization?

Route summarization, also called route aggregation or supernetting, replaces several specific routes with one shorter prefix that covers all of them. A router that learns four /24 networks from a branch can advertise one /22 instead. Routing tables get smaller, updates are sent less often, and a flapping subnet inside the summary no longer causes updates elsewhere in the network.

How the calculation works

  1. Each prefix is converted to a start and end address.
  2. The ranges are sorted, and any that overlap or touch are merged. Duplicates and prefixes already inside a larger one disappear.
  3. Each merged range is turned back into the fewest aligned CIDR blocks. This is the exact, lossless result.
  4. The single summary route is the longest prefix that contains both the lowest and the highest address, which means the leading bits of the two addresses are the same.

Worked example

The four networks 192.168.0.0/24 to 192.168.3.0/24 differ only in the third octet, which runs from 0 to 3. That is the last two bits of that octet, so the first 22 bits are shared and the summary is 192.168.0.0/22. It covers exactly the 1,024 addresses you listed.

Now drop 192.168.3.0/24. The three remaining networks still need a /22 to cover them in one route, but that /22 now includes 256 addresses that are not yours. The calculator shows that as extra addresses, and the minimal list is 192.168.0.0/23 plus 192.168.2.0/24.

When summarization is a bad idea

A summary that covers addresses you do not own or cannot reach can send traffic into a black hole, and it can override a more specific, correct route from another path. Summarize at boundaries where you control the address plan, and give every site a block that lines up on a power of two so it summarizes cleanly. Plan this before you deploy, because renumbering later is painful.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between summarization and supernetting?

They describe the same math. Supernetting is the term for combining classful or classless networks into a shorter prefix, and summarization is the routing term for advertising that combined prefix.

Why can't I summarize 10.0.1.0/24 and 10.0.2.0/24 into a /23?

A /23 must start on an even third octet. 10.0.1.0/23 is not a valid network, because the containing /23 is 10.0.0.0/23. The two blocks sit in different /23s, so the smallest single prefix covering both is 10.0.0.0/22.

Does the summary replace the specific routes?

That depends on the protocol. OSPF area range and BGP summary-only suppress the specifics. EIGRP summaries and static discard routes keep the specifics in the local table. Routers always prefer the longest matching prefix.

No. The calculation runs in your browser.
