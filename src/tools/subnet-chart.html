How to read the chart

The prefix is the number of network bits. The subnet mask writes those bits as four decimal octets, and the wildcard mask is its inverse, which Cisco ACLs and OSPF network statements use. Usable hosts excludes the network and broadcast addresses, except for /31 (two usable addresses on a point-to-point link) and /32 (one host).

Quick subnetting method

  1. Find the interesting octet: the one where the mask is neither 255 nor 0.
  2. Compute the block size: 256 minus the mask value in that octet. For 255.255.255.224 it is 256 − 224 = 32.
  3. Subnets start at multiples of the block size: .0, .32, .64, .96 and so on.
  4. The broadcast address is one less than the next subnet; usable hosts lie between.

Choosing a subnet size

  • /30 or /31: router-to-router links. Prefer /31 where your platforms support RFC 3021.
  • /29 to /28: small DMZs, management networks, internet hand-offs.
  • /27 to /25: server, voice, wireless or guest VLANs.
  • /24: the default access VLAN size for many campuses.
  • /23 to /16: large VLANs, site or data-centre aggregates. Very large broadcast domains hurt performance, so subdivide.
  • /32: loopbacks, router IDs and host routes.

Frequently asked questions

What subnet mask is /24?

255.255.255.0, with a wildcard mask of 0.0.0.255 and 254 usable hosts.

What is the difference between /30 and /31?

A /30 has four addresses with two usable. A /31 has two addresses and, under RFC 3021, both are usable on point-to-point links, saving half the address space per link.

Why are usable hosts two fewer than total addresses?

The first address identifies the subnet itself (network address) and the last is the directed broadcast address, so neither can be assigned to a host.

How do I convert a mask to a prefix length?

Count the 1 bits. 255.255.255.192 is eight, eight, eight and two 1 bits, so it is /26.
