How to choose

Count the devices you have today, add growth, and pick the smallest prefix that fits. A common rule is to plan for 30 to 50 percent more hosts than you currently have. Then check that the subnet also leaves room for the gateway, any HSRP or VRRP addresses, and printers or other fixed-address devices.

The /24 default

A /24 is popular because the mask is easy to read, the last octet is the host number, and 254 hosts suits most VLANs. Bigger broadcast domains cause more broadcast and ARP traffic, so very large flat networks such as a /16 of user devices are normally avoided. If you need many hosts, use several /24 subnets and route between them.

Small subnets

A /30 is the classic point-to-point size, using two of four addresses. A /31 uses both addresses and saves half the space on large networks with many links, as long as the devices support it. A /32 is used for loopbacks, router IDs and host routes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most common subnet size?

/24 for user networks and /30 or /31 for router links.

Why are there only 2 usable hosts in a /30?

A /30 has four addresses. One is the network address and one is the broadcast, which leaves two for hosts.

Can I have a /31 subnet?

Yes, on point-to-point links under RFC 3021. There is no network or broadcast address, so both addresses are usable.
